### Five Proficiency Levels
| Level | Name | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Novice | Can tag statements by altitude when given time and no live pressure; doesn't yet do it inside a real conversation |
| 2 | Practicing | Applies Step 0 and the ladder live, but consciously, as a checklist; still misses the subtler tells of [Module 4](/altitude/module-04)'s failure modes |
| 3 | Fluent | Catches spelunking, blindness, and overuse mid-conversation via their tells; deploys bridge statements naturally, without visibly "running the framework" |
| 4 | Advisory-Capable | Runs vertical traceability ([Module 6](/altitude/module-06)) unprompted; can raise a stenographer-failure concern without damaging the relationship |
| 5 | Facilitator | Can shadow and debrief other practitioners (Drill 3/4), calibrate answer keys, and teach the framework itself |
Most working practitioners should target Level 3–4. Level 5 is specifically the bar for anyone who'll run this training for others.
### Capstone Rubric
Score the [Module 13](/altitude/module-13) capstone against:
- **Tagging accuracy** — correct altitude and speaker attribution throughout
- **L2 construction quality** — bridge statements are genuinely earned (enough L0/L1 material), not reflexive
- **Standing and conflict handling** — provisional/conflicted statements correctly flagged; genuine vs. apparent conflicts correctly distinguished
- **Document structure** — output matches [Module 10](/altitude/module-10)'s structure, status flags present and honest
- **Vertical traceability** — at least one topic genuinely traced, with a real observation, not a pro-forma checkbox
- **Delivery-drift awareness** — the [Module 12](/altitude/module-12) section shows genuine reasoning about what a later statement would need to be checked against
A trainee who scores well on tagging but weak on traceability is a Level 2–3 practitioner, not yet 4 — useful information for where to focus further practice rather than a pass/fail gate.
